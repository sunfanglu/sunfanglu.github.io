3Fault lines that divide the debate
The question the meeting actually stalls on Almost everyone acknowledges that AI has benefits. What remains unresolved is who may set the pace of frontier models, who bears the risks, and whether the Security Council should be the body that deals with this.

Fault line 1 · Pace of the frontier

The scientific panel and some labs want safety evidence to stay ahead of capability. The United States rejects a pause and global speed limits. Several Global South countries agree that the risks cross borders, but reject a rule under which a few companies and states decide who must slow down.

Fault line 2 · Choice of forum

One side wants loss of control, biological misuse, and lethal autonomous weapons kept within the Council’s field of view. The other side insists on specialized bodies, domestic law, and intergovernmental consensus, so that the Council does not become a technology regulator.

Fault line 3 · Who is left out

If the language of catastrophic risk is written only by frontier states, it will be read as a new technology threshold. Access, production, open source, and mineral value chains therefore become security questions, not only development questions.
